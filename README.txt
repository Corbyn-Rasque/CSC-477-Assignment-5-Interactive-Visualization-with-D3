# Writeup

## A rationale for your design decisions. How did you choose your particular visual encodings and interaction techniques? What alternatives did you consider and how did you arrive at your ultimate choices?

The globe was the motivating factor behind the visualization. I very much wanted an interactive way to explore what coffee flavors each country specializes in, as I've noticed over time that I tend to gravitate towards coffee from countries like Columbia & Ethiopia (preferring sweet & floral flavors over funky fermented & chocolate notes).

Using a round globe both invites the viewer to pan around through occlusion of half the earth, as well as offers the ability to put a radial chart around it without occlusion—making good use of space. A stacked chart was influenced directly from the SCAA Coffee Flavor wheel chart, and was used and sized so that interaction could occur without issues around the data 'expanding' and taking up more space or occluding things. Looking through D3 examples, I really liked that the radial sunburst chart offered easy information on demand in this way through labels getting larger when clicked, so this interaction was used here. By making the cursor change when mousing over the globe or past the radial chart, affordance was provided to show the user that the chart could be interacted with. Similarly, country name on hover gives information on demand and indicates that a country can be clicked, with the highlighting offering an easy way to filter data in an intuitive way. Darkening the flavors on selecting a country makes it clear what flavors primarily come from that country, along with highlighting the country the color of the flavor it's best known for. Trends in coffee growing regions and flavors become a bit more clear in this way. Lastly, double clicking to reset was an obvious choice; it's intuitive and gets a frustrated user who's rapidly clicking out of a view.

The flavor chord chart is one that—if I'm being honest—wish showed more. It is built from flavors found in conjunction with another in coffees bags from a country, with the size of the radial space taken up indicating the proportion of bags from that country with that flavor note included. I found out fairly quickly, however, that the dominant flavor notes found together are tied to the most broad categories corresponding roughly to dark (roasted / nutty) and light roast (sweet / floral), with the breakdown being roughly how much of each comes from beans from that country. This is more tied to global coffee consumption trends, though does vary slightly from country to country. My hope was to show distinct types of coffee being sold—whether it be *funky anaerobics* from Columbia or *blueberry bombs* \[sic\] from Ethiopia—but it does give a slight indication of the differences in breakdown by country. Since the filtering is by country, tying it to the globe was obvious.

To be quite honest, this was the visualization I had in my head from the start when I saw the chord chart on D3 examples. The rest came together from the final project (working on the globe) and wanting the SCAA chart to be interactive by country. I very much like the idea of each country having a unique rotary-encoded 'barcode' of sorts, uniquely fingerprinting the style of coffee that comes out of the country. For the chord diagram, it was originally going to be a static display across all coffee producing countries (similar to the Les Miserables visual), but tying it to the country selection extended the barcode concept nicely and added a dimension of information that tells you something about each country.
